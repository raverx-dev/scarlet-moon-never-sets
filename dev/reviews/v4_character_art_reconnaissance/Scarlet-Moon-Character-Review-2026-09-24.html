3 · Defects and constraints that matter

  • Reimu facing and centering: current gameplay uses the side-facing body and separate gohei. The saved back-facing candidate is a useful review starting point, not a finished art direction. Keep player coordinates and hitbox unchanged; anchor the body independently of the asymmetric prop.
  • Frame silhouette continuity: Remilia visibly changes body scale between idle A/B; Cirno and Marisa alternate frames lose portions of the broader silhouette. All inspected rows have consistent lengths: this is visible authored-frame discontinuity, not a ragged-row parsing defect.
  • Edge clipping: gameplay clips actors to x=0–191. Reimu can move to x=8/184 and y=18/230; a 24-pixel-tall body and outboard gohei can reach beyond the playfield near limits. Preserve movement bounds; manage art/prop extents deliberately. Portraits are clipped to a 32×32 interior.
  • Distinct framing: combat has 192×240 plus HUD, story has the full 256×240. Dialogue panel begins at y=174. Reimu is usually at (56,124); partners are Cirno (196,108), Marisa (152,108), Sakuya (208,108), Remilia (200,124). Redraws must fit these staging needs without blindly enlarging actors.
  • Pose triggers are part of the contract: idle A/B use (frame>>4)&1 (16 simulation ticks per pose); focus/fire override Reimu; frozen bullets select Cirno/Sakuya special poses; Marisa m3/m4 and Remilia r5 select attack/final art. Preserve these conditions and timing.
  • Defeat is staged movement, not a separate sprite sheet: it reuses the named boss through the animation selector; Cirno drops/rebounds, Sakuya exits right, Marisa gets particle accents. The boss object remains until after defeat, so existing phase/freeze conditions can still influence pose selection.

5 · Bounded production sequence

  1. Reimu gameplay pilot: review existing back-facing candidate, choose the visual treatment, then authorize only idle A/B, focus/fire and two gohei states. Acceptance: native body/prop anchors, center and edge views, unchanged selector timing. This establishes the reusable export pattern.
  2. Reimu story + portrait: four idle/talk frames and one portrait. Review in introduction, boss dialogue, defeat, Shrine ending and credits. Keep the title drawing for the presentation pass.
  3. Marisa: two idle frames, attack, portrait and separate book prop. Review body/broom continuity across combat, Forest dialogue, defeat, attract and flying credits. Coordinate with Forest art without touching its current PR.
  4. Cirno: idle A/B, freeze and portrait. Review facing toward Reimu, wing/body consistency, defeat movement and frozen credit vignette; pair with the planned Lake work.
  5. Sakuya, then Meiling as separate small units: Sakuya idle/stop/portrait and relevant scenes; Meiling sleeping art at actual 2× gate/credits scale. Neither requires changing the Mansion proof PR.
  6. Remilia: first idle/final/portrait, then a separate parasol/attract unit. Check constant body scale, wing bounds, final-phase readability and all ending transitions.
  7. Presentation closure: Reimu title art, opening/attract and all six credit vignettes. Reuse accepted families and author only the remaining distinct art. Review identity and scale together across the whole cast.

Each unit stops at an Owner-reviewed native candidate before separately authorized runtime integration. Preserve canonical IDs, frame IDs, anchors and exact revision/export hashes. Use frames for genuine pose variants, layers for useful body/prop separation, and separate assets for different canvas sizes. A simple deterministic placement manifest is sufficient; no new scene-editor tooling is proposed.
